L1 · the drummer MoveLive

The only part played with nothing in the headphones. It is the first bed, so its groove is the song's groove — everything above inherits it.

On Move · 30 min

  • New Set. Name it first: Bisonte Club 2 L1 drummer. The name becomes the bundle, the folder and the Live group.
  • Tempo 124, scale F minor — matching SONG.md exactly.
  • Track 1 kick · 2 clap · 3 hats · 4 perc.
  • One off-grid element: nudge the clap a hair late, or a ghost hat at low velocity. Without it the loop will sound like a grid at bar 32.
  • Stay in Standalone. Nothing here needs the computer.

L2 · the bassist MoveLive

Decide before you play

Does the bass lock to the kick or play around it? Write the answer in SONG.md → Decisions. If you can't answer it, the low end will fight itself and no amount of EQ at mix time fixes an indecisive part.

On Move

  • New Set Bisonte Club 2 L2 bassist, tempo 124, F minor.
  • Track 1: load bed_v01.wav from Samples/Bisonte/Bisonte Club 2/ into a sampler and set it looping. This is your headphone mix.
  • Track 2: the bass. Play it three times; keep the take with the best feel, not the cleanest.
  • Check the participation grid: bass is off in INTRO and BREAK. Don't write a line that only makes sense playing constantly.

In Live

  • move-sync.py sets, bundle → Parts/.
  • Open it, drag track 2 only into Bisonte Club 2 v01.als. Delete the bed track — it was scaffolding.
  • Group as L2 BASSIST, assign the instrument: Guitar and Bass, or Surge XT.
  • Save As v02.als — a part landed, so the version bumps.
  • Render bed_v02.wav (now L1+L2), push it.
→ v02.als · bed_v02.wav

L3 · rhythm, then L4 · lead Move

Two Sets in one sitting. They go fast because the bed now carries drums and bass, so there is something real to play against.

L3 rhythm · against bed_v02

  • Chords or a comping part. This fixes the key for everyone above.
  • Sits between bass and lead: 250 Hz – 2 kHz. Don't write it low or it eats the bass.
  • In Live: Electric Keyboards or Expressive Chords. Render bed_v03, push.

L4 lead · against bed_v03

  • Listen for where L3 is busy and play in the holes. This is the one thing that is trivial with the bed and impossible without it.
  • The grid says the lead is silent for the first 48 bars — it enters at B. Write an entrance, not a loop.
  • Surge XT or Odin 2. Render bed_v04, push.
Halfway checkpoint

Four parts, four beds, v04.als. Play bed_v04 on its own: if it already sounds like a track rather than four loops stacked, the method is working. If it sounds crowded, you are about to find out why in session 5.

→ v04.als · bed_v03, bed_v04
4
50 min

L5 · atmosphere, and the first generative round Live

Atmosphere by resampling

  • Don't play something new. Render a bar of L1–L4, drop it into Granulator III, and stretch it into a pad. The texture then belongs to this song and no other.
  • Alternatives: Drone Lab, Inspired by Nature, Cardinal.
  • This is the one layer that plays throughout — it is the room, not a part.

Generators, first look

  • Try Generators by Iftah and MIDI Tools by Philip Meyer on the hat and bass clips before writing any lomctl.js command.
  • Keep ≤ 3 variants per part. A variant survives only if it plays back-to-back with the original without needing a fill.
  • Rename survivors by function — hat A, hat A' — not by seed.
→ v05.als · bed_v05
5
60 min

Arrange to 104 bars Live

Everything so far has been eight-bar loops. This is where it becomes a song, and where the participation grid earns its place.

The order that works

  • Name seven scenes exactly as the form does: INTRO · A · B · BREAK · B' · A' · OUTRO.
  • Lay them out to the bar counts: 16, 16, 16, 8, 16, 16, 16.
  • Now go through the grid row by row and mute what should not be playing. This is the "everybody rests" rule applied in one pass — expect to remove roughly a quarter of everything you recorded.
  • Build L6 last, against the arrangement: a riser into A, tension in the BREAK, fills through B'.
Where it usually goes wrong

The song feels flat and the instinct is to add. It is almost always the BREAK: too long, too busy, or silently full because nothing was muted. B′ only lands if the eight bars before it took something away.

→ v06.als, 104 bars
6
60 min

Mix, render, feed Move LiveMove

Mix

  • Against the REF track at matched loudness, LUFS meter on, limiter off until the end.
  • Low end first — the decision you wrote down in session 2 tells you whether to sidechain or carve.
  • One instrument per part governs playing, not mixing. Double and saturate freely here.

Render and close the loop

# master + stems + seeds for the next song
Renders/master/  Bisonte Club 2 v06.wav
Renders/stems/   L1…L6.wav
Renders/seeds/   drums_8bar.wav, bass_stab.wav,
                 texture.wav

$ move-sync.py push Renders/seeds/*.wav "Bisonte Club 2"

No --tempo here: seeds are one-shots and odd-length loops, not beds, so the 8-bar gate would wrongly refuse them.

One step not yet verified on hardware

Loading bed_v01.wav from Samples/ onto a Move track and looping it is the hinge this whole method turns on, and it has not been done once yet. Prove it in session 2 before building four sessions on top of it — if Move won't loop an 8-bar sample cleanly on a track, the bed has to arrive some other way and the loop changes shape.
